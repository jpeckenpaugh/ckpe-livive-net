CKPE Thesis & Provenance

The severance of knowledge acquisition from knowledge execution in expressive human kinematics.

The CDE Hourglass Architecture

CKPE is a pipeline for compiling expressive human kinematics from paired image/skeleton samples into a deterministic runtime artifact, called a Domain Instruction Set (DIS).

The architecture follows the same shape as the other livive-net projects: the probabilistic work — interrogating data, discovering structure, proposing poses — happens at build time, in an RL scorer and CNN oracle. What survives that interrogation is lowered into a small, integer-friendly, auditable DIS. The runtime never calls a generative model. It executes the compiled grammar.

v1 of CKPE is scoped to a single limb — the right arm — across roughly 100 ASL terms derived from studio keyframes of native signers. The SPA at ckpe.livive.net is the visible surface of that compilation: it shows what the compiler produced, what evidence it produced it from, and how the artifact fits into a broader architecture. It does not claim to understand ASL. It claims to have compiled a kinematic grammar for a constrained subset, and it shows its work.

The Three-Level Gesture Decomposition

Level Description v1 Alpha Status
1. Body Gross kinematics — arm, shoulder, torso, head position In Scope (Right arm)
2. Hands Finger configuration, wrist orientation Deferred to Level 2
3. Face Gaze, mouth morphemes, facial expression Deferred to Level 3

Levels 2 and 3 are deferred to keep the dimensionality of the RL search tractable. The v1 claim is modest: the macroscopic kinematics of the right arm are compiled correctly, and the compilation method generalizes.

Honest Limits & Boundaries (The Appendix)

Retraction of Accuracy Overreach

An earlier draft claimed “the macroscopic kinematics of the right arm are compiled correctly.” That is an overreach and is retracted:

  1. No true-3D ground truth: The alpha set was compiled from single-camera front-view recordings. The reward signal measures how well the projected rig overlaps the observed silhouette mask. It does not measure true 3D joint locations because front-view projection is depth-ambiguous. “Fit to the front view” is a defensible claim; “correct 3D pose” is not.
  2. Not all source poses are reachable: v1 freezes the torso, shoulders, and head. When the source signer shrugged or leaned, the observed hand position is mathematically outside the rig's reachable set. The search finds the nearest wrong answer and reports a lower reward. The solution is the subsequent “tight at the roots” phase (unfreezing the spine and clavicles before solving the arm).
  3. ASL is a structured test corpus, not a translation claim: CKPE uses ASL because it possesses well-segmented gestures, canonical resting states, and studio-controlled footage. CKPE is not an accessibility device or sign translation engine.
  4. Deaf community review pending: Until fluent signers evaluate the compiled sequences, the claim is strictly visual fit to the training sample, not fluency in ASL.
